# Phase 3 novelty review — 16 November 2026

## Decision-relevant finding

The broad claim that format repair can masquerade as self-correction is **directly covered by existing work**. A repository-specific replication, six examples, or a new name for the taxonomy would not establish originality. The literature search is targeted, not exhaustive; preprints are prior art without implying that their results have passed peer review or been independently reproduced here.

## Hypothesis assessment

| Hypothesis | Evidence here | Originality and significance | What is missing; feasibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1: artifact-driven apparent benefit | Seven selected training representation suspects; very strong length-status association in MATH. No causal attribution rate. | Scientifically important, but the broad proposition is substantially anticipated by Calibration Floor. | Trusted MATH keys and symmetric, blinded adjudication of initial/revised answers. CPU/manual work first; a few examples cannot support population claims. |
| H2: cheap finalization recovers verification gains | Not tested causally here. Existing records already include finalizers, complicating interpretation. | Direct prior-art overlap with forced continuation and constrained re-extraction. | A distinct question would need prospective controls of finalizer access, answer preservation and matched realized cost. New calls would be required; not justified before novelty and annotation checks. |
| H3: completion-controlled policy evaluation changes conclusions | Overall MATH gains largely disappear or reverse in stop/nonempty strata; this is descriptive selection by initial state. | Useful audit result, but SEVRA already studies completion and budget allocation. | Independent protocol, reliable answer validity, preregistered strata and uncertainty. Conditioning changes the population; it cannot identify a causal completion effect. |

## Scientific gap worth discussing, not claiming

An independently adjudicated **robustness boundary for policy rankings under extraction uncertainty**, including abstentions and symmetric initial/revised grading, could be a narrower contribution. Another possibility is **non-destructive finalization**: whether finalization itself changes answer content and creates harm, under a prospectively fixed serving protocol. Neither is established as novel by this search. Both require a separate focused literature check before implementation.

No benchmark-contamination diagnosis is possible from these records. Question-disjoint partitions prevent within-experiment leakage; they do not prove the solver never saw benchmark questions in pretraining. Likewise, public raw response text is not a faithful observation of internal model reasoning.

## Reviewer conclusion

The observation is scientifically meaningful as a validity warning, but the proposed broad direction is **not sufficiently distinctive as the principal conference claim**. Prefer a bounded reproducibility/measurement audit to a claim of discovering that parsing can inflate self-correction. Do not invest in new inference merely to reproduce an already explicit prior-art claim.
