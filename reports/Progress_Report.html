This is our secondary analysis, not a claim that we generated these responses or reproduced their published results.

The research idea in simple words

Rechecking can fix an incorrect answer, preserve its correctness, or damage a correct answer. A small model predicts these outcomes from the question and first response. We train it on mathematics and test its decisions on commonsense questions. We then give it a limited number of labeled target examples and measure whether its decisions improve.

Completed

  • Version-pinned real outcome dataset and provenance.
  • Automatic HELP / NO CHANGE / HURT labels.
  • Question-grouped source and target splits.
  • Two lightweight classifiers compared on source development data.
  • Frozen transfer and threshold adaptation at five label budgets.
  • Saved model, results, figures, and Streamlit dashboard.

Next research milestones

  • Collect our own paired maths and reading responses using the included Ollama collector.
  • Repeat across more model checkpoints and partition seeds.
  • Compare probability calibration and small-model retraining against threshold-only adaptation.
  • Investigate feature groups and rare harmful revisions.
  • Compare against random allocation and a longer first solve at matched realized cost.

First empirical result

The frozen source gate reached 76.67% target accuracy versus 76.13% when keeping the first answer and 72.85% when always rechecking. Its harmful-flip rate was 0.00% of target questions. With 200 target questions for threshold adaptation, mean test accuracy was 76.08% (SD 0.38 percentage points across 5 subsets). The completion rule reached 76.67%. Compare the learned gate with this simple rule before attributing improvements to machine learning. None of the tested adaptation budgets improved mean accuracy over the frozen source gate in this pilot.

How leakage is prevented

Every repeated rollout or duplicate question stays in one partition. Correctness labels, revised responses, and rechecking token counts are used for offline labels and evaluation only. They are excluded from prediction features. The source model is chosen on source development data; adaptation uses its own pool.

Limits of this first pilot
  • One solver (Qwen3-4B), one source-to-target direction, one partition seed.
  • Public correctness labels were inherited, not independently rescored; gold answers are absent in the release.
  • Threshold-only adaptation is not full probability calibration or LLM fine-tuning.
  • Five repetitions vary adaptation samples; their standard deviations are not confidence intervals.
  • Target adaptation uses a reserved portion of a formerly published evaluation split under a new protocol; not a reproduction of SEVRA scores.
  • Future correctness and revision responses are never predictor features.
  • No claim of novel method or guaranteed accuracy gains; negative transfer is a valid pilot outcome.

A short explanation for the guide

“I have implemented the first version of a system that predicts whether an LLM should recheck its answer. I trained lightweight models on published maths reasoning outcomes and evaluated transfer to commonsense questions. I also tested how the decision rule changes when given a small amount of labeled data from the new task. The current result is a reproducible public-data pilot. My next step is collecting our own responses on maths and reading benchmarks and validating the findings across models.”
